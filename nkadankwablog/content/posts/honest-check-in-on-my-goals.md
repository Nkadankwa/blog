My goals do not feel as separate as they did when I first wrote them down. Learning Linux, understanding AI, improving as a software developer, and building a homelab now seem like different parts of the same direction: I want to understand the systems I use well enough to build useful things with them.

That connection is encouraging. Nextcloud gave self-hosting a practical purpose. Docker and environment variables stopped being tutorial vocabulary once I needed them for real services. Deploying Eventally exposed questions about row-level security that did not appear while doin different projects in the classroom. Each project has made the next goal more specific.

## Progress does not always look finished

I can also feel the distance between experimenting with a subject and understanding it confidently. There is always another Linux concept, programming pattern, deployment problem, or AI tool to investigate. As a student, that unfinished list can make genuine progress feel smaller than it is.

The better evidence is in what I can now ask. I am no longer only following steps to make a service start. I am thinking about where data lives, how access should work, what should be backed up, and how I would recover. When AI produces code, I am asking whether it matches my design rather than treating a working answer as the end of the problem.

## What I want from the goals now

I do not need every goal to become a race towards expertise. Before graduation, I want strong foundations in Linux, a practical understanding of AI, and enough software experience to keep learning without feeling lost whenever the environment changes.

The homelab and this blog help because they leave evidence. A service that works, a mistake I can explain, or an editorial that makes an idea clearer is a small result I can point to.

So this check-in is hopeful, but not finished. I am pleased that the goals are becoming real projects, and I am aware of how much remains. For now, that feels healthier than either pretending I have arrived or acting as if none of the progress counts.
