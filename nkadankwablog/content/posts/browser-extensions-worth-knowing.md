Browser extensions can make the web feel calmer, safer, or simply less repetitive. They can also request broad access to what I read and type, so I am trying to be more selective about which ones earn a permanent place in my browser.

## The shortlist I would investigate first

### uBlock Origin

uBlock Origin is useful for reducing distracting advertising and unwanted page elements. I like that it makes the web easier to read, not only faster. It is worth checking the official listing and keeping the filter lists close to their defaults until I understand what changing them does.

### Privacy Badger

Privacy Badger is designed to reduce tracking behaviour across websites. It is a good reminder that privacy tools are not magic invisibility cloaks; they reduce one kind of exposure and should sit alongside sensible account, password, and browser habits.

### Consent-O-Matic

Cookie banners make the web tiring. Consent-O-Matic can help express a chosen preference automatically on supported sites. Before using a tool like this, I would still read its privacy policy and make sure I understand what information it needs to do its job.

### Firefox Relay

Firefox Relay offers email aliases, which can be helpful when a site needs an address but I do not want every newsletter or account tied to my primary inbox. The useful part is not hiding from every message; it is making it easier to see who shared or misused an address.

## What I check before installing anything

- Is the extension actively maintained and installed from its official store listing?
- What permissions does it request, and do those permissions match its purpose?
- Is the privacy policy clear about data collection?
- Would I still use it after the first week, or is it adding another icon without solving a real problem?

Extensions are small tools with a lot of access. The best ones make a clear improvement without asking for more of my browsing life than they need.
