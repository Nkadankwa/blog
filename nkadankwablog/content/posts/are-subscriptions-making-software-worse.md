I strongly dislike how subscriptions have spread through software and entertainment. Paying every month can make sense when a service has continuous costs, but too many subscriptions turn access into something I am only borrowing.

Music streaming makes that feeling clear. I can listen to an album repeatedly and download it inside the app, yet if I stop paying I lose local access. The download was never a copy I owned; it was temporary permission. Streaming offers an enormous library and ongoing licensing has real costs, but the convenience should not be confused with ownership.

## When the model makes sense

Servers, storage, new content, support, security work, and licensed catalogues cost money to maintain. A subscription can fund a service that keeps changing and provide a lower starting price than a large purchase. I do not expect every online service to run forever on one payment.

The model becomes harder to accept when software that could work locally stops working because a billing period ended, or when ordinary features are divided into tiers designed to make leaving painful. The customer keeps paying without building any lasting right to use the product.

## Paying to use the internet I already have

PlayStation Plus is particularly difficult for me to justify when online multiplayer is placed behind the subscription. I have already bought the console, the game, and an internet connection. Charging another recurring fee to use the game's online mode feels less like funding a new product and more like controlling a gate.

Subscriptions are not automatically bad, but they should match an ongoing service and make the terms of ownership clear. I want more options: buy software outright, pay for major upgrades, purchase music I can keep, or subscribe only when the continuing service is worth it. Software becomes worse when the subscription is not a useful choice but the only door left open.
