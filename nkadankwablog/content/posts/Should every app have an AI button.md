My answer is no: an AI feature should solve a real problem, not appear in every app because it is the current trend.

I use AI tools often enough to understand the appeal. They can explain an unfamiliar term, help me form a better search query, or point out a gap in how I have described a problem. That usefulness does not mean I want the same assistant placed at the centre of every app I open.

## A feature should begin with the task

In a note-taking app, an AI assistant may help some people organise or summarise information. It may also add cost, resource use, privacy questions, and an interface that distracts from taking notes. In a messaging app such as Snapchat, I am even less convinced that an AI feature belongs at the centre of the experience.

When I open a notes app, I usually want to capture a thought quickly and trust that I can find it later. An AI feature that makes those two things easier may earn its place. One that adds a prominent button, sends private text to a remote model, or complicates a previously simple interface has created new questions before solving the old ones.

The same feature can be useful for one person and unnecessary for another. That is why it should be optional, clearly labelled, and honest about what data leaves the device. If disabling it leaves an empty space or repeated prompts to turn it back on, it was never designed as an optional feature in spirit.

## Education is where I see real potential

Learning platforms are a better example. A well-designed assistant can act like a tutor: explain a concept in another way, create practice questions, or help a learner notice where they are stuck. Even there, it should be optional and should not replace the work of checking sources or thinking through the problem.

The best version would guide rather than simply produce an answer. It could ask me to explain my reasoning, give a smaller hint, or suggest the part of the documentation I should read. The worst version would complete the work so smoothly that I reach the correct screen without understanding how I got there.

I have already learned this lesson from coding tutorials: a working result can hide a missing foundation. AI can make that problem better by adapting an explanation, or worse by removing the productive struggle entirely.

## My standard for the button

The question is not whether AI is impressive. The question is whether it improves the task without making the app heavier, more confusing, or less private for people who never asked for it.

Before adding an AI button, I would ask what repeated problem it solves, what happens to the user’s data, whether the feature can fail safely, and whether the ordinary version of the app remains complete without it. If those answers are weak, the button is probably serving the trend more than the person using the software.
