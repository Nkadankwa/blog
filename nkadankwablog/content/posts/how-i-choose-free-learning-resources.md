There is so much free learning material that finding a course is rarely the hardest part. The harder part is choosing something useful and staying with it long enough to learn.

My mix usually includes auditing Coursera courses, watching YouTube, and using LLMs or Perplexity to search and clarify ideas. Each tool plays a different role.

## Structure, demonstrations, and questions

An audited Coursera course gives me an order. Even when a certificate or graded feature is paid, the available lessons can show which concepts belong together and what should come next.

YouTube is useful when I need to see something done. A database diagram, Linux command, or programming setup can become clearer when I watch the process. I compare more than one explanation when a video skips the reason behind its steps.

LLMs and Perplexity help when I do not yet know the right phrase to search. I can describe the confusion in ordinary language, ask for terminology, then follow the answer towards documentation and other sources. They are helpful guides, not automatic authorities; links still need to be opened and claims checked.

## The filter is the problem I am solving

I try not to save resources just because they look valuable. I ask whether the resource matches my current level, includes practice, explains its assumptions, and helps with something I am building or trying to understand. If I cannot describe what I expect to learn, adding another bookmark will not fix the problem.

Free does not mean costless. Every course takes time and attention. The best free resource for me is not the one with the most hours of content. It is the one that helps me take the next clear step and then gives me enough understanding to work without it.
