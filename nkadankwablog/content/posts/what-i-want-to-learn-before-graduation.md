Before I graduate, I want a solid understanding of Linux and a practical understanding of AI. Both goals are broad, so I need them to mean more than being familiar with the words.

## Linux beyond copying commands

For Linux, I want to understand the system well enough to investigate problems instead of depending entirely on a tutorial. That includes files and permissions, processes, services, networking, logs, packages, storage, and the shell. My homelab gives those topics a purpose because a service that fails makes me use them together.

I do not need to memorise every command. I want to know where to look, how to read what the system is telling me, and how to make a change I can reverse. Being comfortable rebuilding and documenting a small server would mean more to me than completing another disconnected list of exercises.

## AI without treating it as magic

For AI, “to some extent” means understanding enough to use the tools responsibly and judge their output. I want a clearer model of what current systems do well, where they fail, how data and prompting affect an answer, and what verification looks like in coding and research.

I am interested in using AI, including local tools, but I do not want convenience to replace the skills I am trying to learn. If a tool writes code, I should be able to read and test it. If it summarises a source, I should still check the source.

Graduation is a useful deadline for building foundations. I want to leave with enough understanding to keep learning deliberately rather than starting from zero whenever a server fails or a new AI product appears.
