There is almost always something else I could be doing. As a student, finishing one task often reveals the next assignment, topic, or project waiting behind it. If I use an empty task list as permission to rest, I may never rest at all.

I am learning to use different signals.

One is reaching a real stopping point. After completing an assignment, solving a stubborn problem, or finishing a feature, taking a break lets the accomplishment register before I rush into the next thing. Rest becomes part of closing the task.

The other signal appears while I am learning. I can sometimes remain at the desk, continue reading, and still know that very little is going in. I reread the same paragraph, repeat the same error, or follow a tutorial without understanding why the steps work.

## A break is not abandoning the work

Stepping away can feel irresponsible when the work is unfinished. In practice, a short walk, a meal, sleep, or time spent doing something unrelated can make the next attempt better. The problem often looks smaller when I return because my attention is no longer trapped in the same loop.

I still have to be honest with myself. There is a difference between resting because my concentration is gone and avoiding the discomfort of starting. A useful break has a boundary and a reason; endless scrolling usually leaves me less rested than before.

I do not have a perfect schedule for this. The habit I want is simply to notice when effort has stopped producing learning. There will always be another thing to do. That is exactly why I need to decide when enough is enough for today.
