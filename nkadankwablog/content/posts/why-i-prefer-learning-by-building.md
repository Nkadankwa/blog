I remember ideas more easily when I use them to build something. Reading gives me vocabulary and direction, while the project forces me to retrieve that knowledge and make decisions with it.

I read a lot, so some details disappear unless I meet them again. Building creates more ways to remember. I recall the feature I was trying to make, the error that blocked it, the documentation I opened, and the change that finally worked. The concept becomes attached to an experience.

## A project makes gaps visible

While reading, an explanation can feel clear because every step is already arranged. A project removes that arrangement. I have to decide what comes first, connect several topics, and recognise the point where my understanding ends.

That happened with deployment, environment variables, databases, Linux, and Docker. Each subject became more concrete when a project depended on it. A failure also gave me a reason to understand the surrounding system instead of memorising a single fix.

## Building still needs deliberate study

Projects can teach bad habits when I accept the first solution that runs. I still need documentation, structured courses, and explanations that cover concepts my project may never expose. After solving a problem, I try to return to the underlying idea and ask whether the solution is safe, maintainable, and repeatable.

A project that tries to include every new skill can become too large to finish. A small result gives me a tighter feedback loop and a clearer record of what I learned.

Learning by building works for me and I see no reason to change it
