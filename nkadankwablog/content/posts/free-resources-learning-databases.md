Learning databases is easier for me when I can move between seeing the structure, practising the syntax, and hearing an idea explained in another way. Three free resources fill those roles: dbdiagram, SoloLearn, and YouTube.

## dbdiagram: making relationships visible

dbdiagram helps me turn tables and keys into something I can inspect. That fits the way I already plan a schema on paper. Once I describe the tables, I can see relationships, notice a missing key, and discuss the design before building the database itself.

The diagram does not decide whether the model is correct. A clean-looking relationship can still represent the wrong business rule. Its value is making the rule visible enough to question.

## SoloLearn: small pieces of practice

SoloLearn is useful when I want a structured introduction or a short exercise. Small lessons reduce the friction of starting and help me repeat basic SQL ideas. I would not treat completing a course as proof that I can design or operate a real database, but it provides a path through concepts that might otherwise feel disconnected.

## YouTube: another explanation when one does not click

YouTube gives me demonstrations and different teaching styles. I can watch someone build a query, explain a join visually, or walk through normalisation. The quality varies, so I compare explanations, check dates and database versions, and return to official documentation for exact behaviour.

These resources work best together when they lead back to a project. I can sketch a schema in dbdiagram, practise the SQL concept in a structured lesson, watch an explanation of the difficult part, and then implement it myself. The free resource is not the education on its own. The learning happens when I use it to make and test a decision.
