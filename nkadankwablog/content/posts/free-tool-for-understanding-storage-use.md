Windows can tell me that a drive is getting full without making the cause immediately obvious. WizTree gives me a faster way to see where the space went.

The application scans a drive and presents folders and files by size. Its visual map makes large items stand out, while the list view lets me move through the folder structure. This is useful when the problem comes from an old download, a forgotten installer, a virtual machine, a game, or project files spread across several locations.

## Seeing size changes the cleanup decision

Searching manually encourages me to inspect the folders I already suspect. A storage map can reveal that my assumption was wrong. A directory I barely remember may be bigger than the one I planned to clean.

I am especially careful around Windows folders and unfamiliar data. Deleting the largest item blindly can create a much larger problem than low disk space.

## A focused addition to Windows

WizTree earns a place in my toolkit because it answers one question quickly: which files and folders are taking the space? It gives me evidence before I start moving or deleting anything.
