A self-hosted application earns a permanent place when it solves a real problem and respects the data I give it.

Privacy is part of that decision. I do not want an application broadcasting information unnecessarily or making hidden external connections for features I never asked for. Self-hosting gives me more control over where data lives, though I still need to inspect integrations, telemetry, update sources, and any external APIs the application uses.

## Purpose comes first

The best application focuses on its main job. Extra dashboards, AI buttons, social features, and integrations can make the interface heavier without improving the reason I installed it. I value a service that handles its core task reliably and explains its settings clearly.

Actual use matters more than the excitement of installation. A service I open once and then maintain for months has become a responsibility without remaining useful. Updates, storage, backups, passwords, and troubleshooting all consume time.

## Keeping it has a continuing cost

I ask whether the application remains useful enough to justify that work. I also consider whether I can export its data, recover after a failure, and understand the upgrade process. Good documentation and an active project make the decision easier.

Removing a service does not mean the experiment failed. Testing it may have taught me about Docker, permissions, databases, or my own workflow. The lesson can remain after the container is gone.

The applications worth keeping are the ones I trust and continue to use. They solve a clear problem, keep control understandable, and avoid turning maintenance into the main activity. A smaller set of purposeful services gives me more value than a crowded dashboard of things I installed because they looked interesting.
