“Free” cloud storage is useful, but it is rarely free in every sense. Usually there is a storage cap, a feature limit, advertising, a subscription upsell, or a reason for the provider to keep me inside its ecosystem.

That does not make free storage dishonest. It does mean I need to understand what I am trading and what happens if the rules change.

## The question behind the question

The important question is not only how many gigabytes I get. It is whether I can retrieve my files easily, export them in ordinary formats, understand the privacy settings, and keep a second copy somewhere else.

Changes to storage policies are a useful reminder that a service I rely on can change its limits, pricing, or direction. I should not build my only copy of important photos, notes, or project files around an assumption that a free tier will stay the same forever.

## The routine I am trying to build

- Keep important files in a format I can open without one specific service.
- Maintain another copy on a device or storage location I control.
- Check account storage before it becomes an emergency.
- Read policy-change emails instead of assuming they are unimportant.

Cloud storage is still convenient. I just want convenience to be part of a backup plan, not the entire plan.
