## Three places I return to when I am stuck

When I get stuck, my first instinct can be to search for the exact error and hope someone has already solved it. That is useful in the moment, but it does not always leave me better prepared for the next problem. The learning websites I remember are the ones that change how I approach the subject after I close the tab.

LabEx, CS50, and VisuAlgo each help me in a different way. I have not completed everything they offer, and this is not a ranking. They are three places I return to depending on whether I need to practise, rebuild a foundation, or finally see an abstract idea move.

### LabEx

LabEx became useful while I was learning Linux. Reading a command is not the same as typing it, seeing the output, making a mistake, and working out what changed. The guided lab environment lowers the cost of experimentation: I can practise without worrying that I am damaging the machine I rely on.

What I take back to my own setup is confidence, not just commands. After repeating a task in a disposable environment, I am more comfortable reading the manual or trying a careful variation instead of copying the next answer blindly.

### CS50

CS50 is where I go when the problem is not really the framework in front of me. A bug may appear in Java, JavaScript, or a Spring Boot project, but the missing idea can be much more basic: how data is represented, how control flows, or why one approach scales differently from another.

The course’s energy helps, but the real value for me is the way it treats fundamentals as interesting rather than as a hurdle to rush through. It reminds me that returning to a basic concept is not moving backwards.

### VisuAlgo

VisuAlgo helps when code feels too still. A list of steps for sorting or traversing a structure can make sense sentence by sentence while the whole process remains blurry. Watching values move and seeing the current operation highlighted gives me a mental picture I can carry back to the code.

I used to look for one perfect platform that would teach me everything. These sites are more useful because they do not try to play the same role. LabEx lets me practise, CS50 strengthens the ideas underneath the tools, and VisuAlgo makes invisible processes visible.

The website that teaches me something is not always the one with the quickest answer. It is the one that leaves me able to ask a better question when I get stuck again.
