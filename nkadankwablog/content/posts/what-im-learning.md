Right now, I am trying to become more comfortable with the space between following a tutorial and understanding a system.
That includes Linux commands, Docker, self-hosted tools, local AI experiments, and the foundations behind software projects. With Linux, I am practising files, permissions, package management, and reading error messages before searching for a command to paste. With Docker, I am learning that images, volumes, ports, networks, and environment variables all affect whether a setup stays reliable.
The individual topics are different, but the pattern is the same: I understand more when I slow down, test one small thing, and write down why it worked.
What is getting clearer
I am beginning to recognise the roles inside a stack instead of seeing every container or configuration file as one mysterious block. A database has a job. A network has a job. Environment variables, storage paths, and API keys all exist for reasons. I do not know everything yet, but I am less likely to treat a working tutorial as the end of the learning.
What is still difficult
Networking, storage, and debugging remain the parts that make me pause. They are also the parts I want to understand better, because they are where a project becomes more than a screen that happens to load.
The aim is not to race through tools. It is to keep building enough confidence that the next problem feels like something I can investigate rather than something I need to avoid.
